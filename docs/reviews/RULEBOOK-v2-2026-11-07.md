# Battle Ostriches rulebook, version 2 (proposed), 2026-11-07

Changes from v1 follow Brian's decisions after the fresh review (RULEBOOK-REVIEW-2026-11-07.md).

This is the proposed rule set in neutral form, for review. It is not what is live. Items marked **[OPEN]** are still being tested. Numbers in [brackets] are tuning values.

## 1. Terms

- **Board:** 9 columns by 7 rows of squares.
- **Distance:** king-move distance: the larger of the column difference and the row difference. "Next to" means distance 1.
- **Bird:** the ostrich or the vulture. **Hunter:** the hyena or the badger. **Animal:** any of these.
- **Side:** the ostrich side (the ostrich, or both ostriches in 2 vs 2), the vulture side (one or two vultures), each hunter.
- **Leaf:** one of two fixed food squares. **Rock pile:** one of two fixed squares where the vulture gets stones.
- **Wrecked square:** a square a stone has landed on (see 6). Leaves and rock piles are never wrecked.
- **Buried:** a badger that has dug in (see 7.4).
- **Move:** an animal's own action on its own turn. **Push:** being moved by something else (see 5).
- **Catch:** see 4.

## 2. Game types and turn order

One round = every animal takes one turn, in this order:

| Game type | Players | Turn order each round |
|---|---|---|
| Classic | 2 | vulture, ostrich |
| 3 players | 3 | vulture, ostrich, hunter (hyena or badger) |
| Hunters | 4 | vulture, ostrich, hyena, badger |
| 2 vs 2 | 4 | vulture 1, ostrich 1, vulture 2, ostrich 2 |
| 2 vs 2 + hunter | 5 | vulture 1, ostrich 1, vulture 2, ostrich 2, hunter |

Computers can fill any seat. The game lasts [30] rounds unless it ends earlier (see 9).

**Setup:** the ostrich hides [3] eggs ([2] per ostrich in 2 vs 2), not on a rock pile and not on or next to a leaf, then picks its starting square. The vulture and hunters start on fixed squares.

## 3. Moving (every animal)

3.1 An animal moves in any direction, diagonals included, up to [2] squares (distance). Exception: an ostrich carrying an egg moves up to [1].
3.2 It may pass over anything (animals, eggs, wrecked squares). Only the landing square matters.
3.3 It may not land on a wrecked square. A bird may not land on a square holding an animal it can see. A hunter may land on a bird (a catch, 4) or on the other hunter (a meeting, 8). Anyone may land on something it cannot see (a hidden egg; a buried badger, see 7.4). An animal may also stay where it is instead of moving (for the ostrich this is sitting if it is on an egg).
3.4 **Your square works first.** When an animal lands by its own move, the landing square's effect happens first (ostrich: eat on a leaf, warm an egg it stands on; vulture: refill on a rock pile, find an egg it lands on, sniff), then any catch (4).

## 4. Catches (every bird, both hunters)

4.1 A catch happens when a hunter lands on a bird, or a bird lands on a hunter (including a buried badger it could not see).
4.2 A caught bird scrambles: it is pushed [2] squares (5) in a random direction chosen by the game among the 8 straight directions whose landing square is open (not wrecked, no animal that is up). If none is open at 2, a random open square 1 away; if none, the hunter steps back 1 instead. It plays its next turn normally. (A later version lets the bird's player choose.)
4.3 The [2nd] catch on the same bird knocks it out: it leaves the board for the rest of the game.
4.4 A hunter never learns the square a caught bird scrambled to; it keeps only its normal sense (7.3, 7.4). Because the direction is random, it cannot work it out.
4.5 An ostrich carrying an egg when caught drops the egg on the square where it was caught (even if that square is next to a leaf).

## 5. Pushes

5.1 A push moves an animal straight along a line for a fixed number of squares. When the two animals share a square, the direction is the direction of the move that brought them together (forward if the animal already there is pushed, backward if the mover is pushed); for a move that is not straight, the direction of its longer part.
- caught bird: [2], random direction (4.2);
- hunters meeting: [2], the one moved onto is pushed away from the mover (see 8);
- a stone wrecking an occupied square: [2], away from the vulture (6.4);
- the ostrich's Kick: [3], or [2] for the badger (7.1).
5.2 A push stops early on the last open square before a wrecked square, the board edge or any animal that is up (seen or not). If it cannot move at all, the pushed animal stays and the pusher steps back 1 instead. Exception: a Kick that reaches the board edge continues from the opposite edge (both directions for diagonals) and always travels its full distance; it still stops before a wrecked square or an animal that is up. The kick preview shows the direction and distance and says it may stop early; it never reveals an animal the ostrich cannot see.
5.3 A buried badger does not stop a push: the pushed animal ends on top of it and nothing happens (5.4); the badger stays buried.
5.4 **Being pushed is not a move.** Nothing that happens on arrival happens (eating, finding, refilling, sniffing, catching). Where the animal ends up still counts for where it is (an ostrich pushed onto its own egg stands on it, so it warms and guards that egg).
5.5 A push applied to a buried badger itself (a stone, 6.4) brings it up.

## 6. Stones (vulture) and wrecked squares

6.1 The vulture holds at most [1] stone. Landing on a rock pile by its own move refills it.
6.2 On its turn, instead of flying, the vulture may throw its stone at its own square or a square next to it, but never at its teammate's square in 2 vs 2.
6.3 A stone hits whatever is on its square, seen or not, all at once:
- the ostrich's square: the ostrich kicks the stone back (6.5) and nothing else happens;
- an egg not under the ostrich is smashed;
- any other animal there (a hunter, a buried badger) is pushed [2] away from the vulture; a buried badger comes up;
- an ordinary square is wrecked; a leaf or rock pile is never wrecked (the stone is simply used up).
6.4 If the vulture's stone lands on its own square (to smash an egg under it, or kicked back), it hops to the nearest open square (ties: farthest from the ostrich).
6.5 **Kicked back:** a stone thrown at the ostrich's square lands on the vulture's own square instead and acts as a normal stone there (6.3, 6.4). If the vulture stands on a leaf or rock pile, the stone continues one more square straight past the vulture and lands there as a normal stone; if that square is off the board, a leaf or a rock pile, the stone is used up. A kicked-back stone is never kicked back again: if it lands on an ostrich, it is used up.

## 7. Each animal

### 7.1 Ostrich

- **Speed:** [2]; [1] when carrying.
- **Carry:** move onto an adjacent allowed square holding an egg in its beak (not onto another egg, a rock pile, a wrecked square, or any square on or next to a leaf). The ostrich can never put an egg on or next to a leaf. The egg is set down under the ostrich before its next turn. An egg in the beak is not warmed.
- **Sit:** stay on an egg instead of moving. No limit on sitting. Anyone who can see the ostrich can see when it is sitting on an egg.
- **Kick:** instead of moving, push one animal next to it that it can see: [3] squares, or [2] for the badger (5). The ostrich stays put (if on an egg, it keeps standing on it). Not on a turn it carries; never a buried badger; never a teammate in 2 vs 2. The landing square is shown before the kick is confirmed.
- **Needs:** eat (land on a leaf by its own move, or stay on one) at least every [15] of its turns or it starves (is knocked out). Each egg goes cold (is lost) unless the ostrich stands or sits on it at least once every [15] rounds.
- **Sees:** both birds always; hunters within [2] except a buried badger.

### 7.2 Vulture

- **Speed:** flies [2].
- **Sniff:** when it lands by its own move, it learns whether any egg is on or next to its square (yes or no).
- **Find:** landing by its own move on a square with a hidden egg reveals that egg to the vulture.
- **Stone:** see 6.
- **Sees:** both birds always; hunters within [2] except a buried badger.

### 7.3 Hyena

- **Speed:** walks [2].
- **Sense:** at the start of its turn it learns whether any bird is within [2]; if yes, every square where a bird could be is marked as the area where a bird may be.
- **Pounce:** if a bird was sensed at the start of its turn and its move did not catch, it senses again and may move [1] more square. On the second reading, squares inside both readings are marked as the most likely area.
- **Sees:** birds only through its sense; the other hunter within [2] except a buried badger.

### 7.4 Badger

- **Speed:** walks [2].
- **Sense:** as the hyena (the area where a bird may be).
- **Burrow:** staying still for one turn buries it, on any square it can stand on, including a leaf or rock pile. Buried: no bird can see it; it sees any bird next to it exactly (ambush sight); every player is told "The badger dug in!"; birds that could see it when it dug in see the spot. A bird landing on it is caught (4.1). A hyena may land on it unknowingly; they share the square and nothing happens.
- **Coming up:** moving brings it up ("The badger came out of its burrow!" to everyone). If a hyena stands on top, it may burst out instead of moving normally, pushing the hyena [2] back the way the hyena came. Any push also brings it up (5.5).
- **Sees:** as the hyena, plus ambush sight while buried.

## 8. Hunters meeting

8.1 A hunter that moves onto the other hunter's square (when it can see it) pushes that hunter [2] away (5). Nobody is caught.
8.2 A hyena moving onto a buried badger it cannot see: they share the square (7.4).

## 9. Endings (checked in this order whenever something changes)

9.1 **A knocked-out bird loses.** Being knocked out does not reveal hidden eggs or hunters while the game continues; the phone remains read-only with only its normal information. Its side is out once all its birds are knocked out (in 2 vs 2, a team stays in while one of its birds is in). The game goes on for everyone still in.
9.2 **Both birds (all birds) knocked out:** the hunter wins at once. In Hunters mode: more knock-outs wins; if equal, more catches; if still equal, a tie.
9.3 **No egg left** (smashed or cold) while the vulture side is in: the vulture side wins.
9.4 **An egg is alive after round [30]** while the ostrich side is in: the ostrich side wins.
9.5 **Classic:** a knocked-out bird ends the game; the other side wins.
9.6 **Time runs out and no side still in has met its goal:** a draw.

## 10. What each animal can see (summary)

| Viewer | Ostrich | Vulture | Hyena | Badger (up) | Badger (buried) | Eggs |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ostrich | always | always | within [2] | within [2] | the dig-in spot only if it saw the dig | its own eggs (in 2 vs 2 / 5 players, both ostriches see both nests) |
| Vulture | always | always | within [2] | within [2] | the dig-in spot only if it saw the dig | only eggs it found (in 2 vs 2 / 5 players, both vultures share found eggs) |
| Hyena | where a bird may be, only | where a bird may be, only | — | within [2] | never | never |
| Badger | where a bird may be; exact if next to it while buried | same | within [2] | — | — | never |

**Finished-game look back:** once the whole game is over, every player sees the full board, including all eggs and animals. This does not apply to a bird knocked out while others are still playing.

## 11. Announcements

Every phone is told about every big event (a catch, a knock-out, a kick, the hunters meeting, the badger digging in or coming out, a stone kicked back). Where it happened is shown only to players who can see that square. Hunters never see where a caught bird lands (4.4).

## 12. Design goals (for the reviewer)

Every animal has one speed, one skill and its own way of seeing; everything else is universal. Rules must be understandable to a 10-year-old from a short role card. Every player still in the game keeps a way to win; a knocked-out player is out. No result appears without a visible cause. Fun matters as much as fairness (a fair share is 50% in 2-sided games, 33% in 3-sided, 25% in Hunters).
